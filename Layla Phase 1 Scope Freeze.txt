1. PURPOSE
================================================================================
This document defines, in writing and before development commences, the complete
and final scope of Phase 1 of the Layla Campaign Orchestration & Customer Lifecycle
Engine. It converts the Phase 1 feature list into concrete, testable deliverables
and acceptance criteria.

On signature by both parties, Phase 1 scope is frozen. Development proceeds against
this document only. Any feature, modification, or addition outside this scope is
treated as a change request and is quoted separately (see Section 11).

Phase 2 and Phase 3 remain outlined but not contracted, and are scoped in detail
after the preceding phase is delivered.

4. PHASE 1 DELIVERABLES
================================================================================
The following features are delivered, tested, and accepted under Phase 1:

4.1 Campaign Creator
- Create, name, and configure campaigns from the dashboard.
- Campaign name and objective are configurable.
- Campaign start date and end date are configurable.
- Campaign is targeted by selecting contacts/groups using the existing
  contact/broadcast system.

4.2 Multi-Step Follow-Up Sequences
- A configurable sequence of steps per campaign (e.g. Day 0 -> Day 2 -> Day 5 -> Day 10 -> Day 21).
- Each step sends a different approved WhatsApp template.
- Sequences can be configured and changed after creation without development work
  (configuration, not new code).

4.3 Automatic Sequence Progression
- If a customer does not respond, the system automatically sends the next sequence
  step at the configured interval.

4.4 Response Detection
- When a customer responds to a campaign message, the system detects the response,
  stops that customer's sequence, and routes them into the existing sales flow.
- The response, response time, and outcome are recorded (see 4.7).

4.5 "Not Interested" Reason Capture
- When a customer indicates they are not interested, Layla attempts to identify
  the reason through conversation (price, already has service, not needed, not now,
  needs more info, competitor, not eligible, other) and stores it on the customer profile.

4.6 Intent Classification
- Layla classifies every response: interested, not interested, already has service,
  needs information, no response, other.
- Where classification is uncertain, the system defaults to "uncertain -> human review"
  rather than misclassifying.
- Expected initial accuracy ~85-90%, improving with tuning.

4.7 Interaction Tracking
- Every campaign message is tracked: who was contacted, when, delivery status,
  whether they responded, response time, outcome.
- A full audit trail of who was contacted, when, and what happened is stored and
  queryable.
- Failed message visibility: Any campaign message that fails to deliver (or for
  which WhatsApp returns an error) is logged with its failure status, identifiable,
  and visible to the administrator in the campaign dashboard, so delivery failures
  can be monitored and addressed. (Note: automatic retry of failed messages remains
  out of Phase 1 scope.)

4.8 Campaign Dashboard
- View all active campaigns.
- See how many contacts are at each sequence step.
- Monitor campaign progress.

4.9 Campaign Performance Report
- Per-campaign statistics: Messages sent, Delivery rate, Response rate, Number entering
  the sales flow, Number converted. (Metric definitions agreed in Section 8.)

4.10 Contact List Targeting
- Select which contacts/groups to target for each campaign (uses the existing
  broadcast contact system).

4.11 Customer Profile Extension
- Lead profiles extended with: campaign history, last contact date, last response,
  rejection reason.

4.12 No-Response Flagging
- Customers who do not respond after the final sequence step are flagged for nurture
  (the flag feeds Phase 2 nurture campaigns; Phase 2 itself is not part of Phase 1).


================================================================================
5. MULTIPLE & SIMULTANEOUS CAMPAIGNS
================================================================================
- Layla can run multiple campaigns simultaneously (e.g. a Telkom Fibre Promotion to
  one group and a Telkom LTE Promotion to another).
- Targeting is manual in Phase 1: the appropriate campaign is assigned per contact
  list/group at setup.
- Automatic rules-based segmentation / dynamic campaign assignment is Phase 2 and is
  not included.


================================================================================
6. DUPLICATE & CONFLICTING MESSAGES
================================================================================
- Duplicate sends (the same message sent twice to the same customer within a sequence)
  are prevented by sequence/send tracking.
- Automatic conflict prevention across overlapping campaigns (campaign priority,
  exclusion rules, "customers never receive contradictory messages") is Phase 2 and
  is not included.
- In Phase 1, a customer is enrolled in one active campaign at a time per list;
  targeting is managed by the client via lists. If one contact appears in two different
  groups that are both run, Phase 1 does not automatically arbitrate between them.

7. PHASE 1 EXTENDED CONTROLS & VISIBILITY BUNDLE
================================================================================
The three items below are added to Phase 1 as bounded, basic additions.

7.1 Basic Manual Controls
- Manual status override: An administrator can set a customer's status manually.
- Manual classification correction: An administrator can correct a specific response
  classification; the correction is stored against the customer and applied to tune
  classification prompts going forward.
- Manual campaign removal: An administrator can remove a customer from a campaign
  immediately, stopping further scheduled messages.
- Audit trail for manual changes: Every manual change to a customer's status or
  classification is recorded with an audit trail showing the original value, the
  amended value, the user who made the change, and the date/time of the change.
- Boundary: This is a manual correction interface. The correction is stored and used
  to refine prompts. The full self-improving/automated re-learning pipeline is
  excluded (later phase).

7.2 Basic, Robust Backups
- Automated daily database backups with retention beyond the free-tier limit.
- A documented restore path for customer, campaign, and interaction data, tested
  once at handover.
- Boundary: Basic but robust - daily backups plus a restore path. Formal disaster
  recovery, defined RPO/RTO targets, and recovery drills are excluded (later phase).
  Enabled on Supabase Pro (ongoing cost, Section 2).

7.3 Basic Customer Journey Visibility
- A simple, customer-level "what happened" record view: for a selected customer,
  show campaign(s), current step/status, last response, classification, and outcome
  (i.e. Campaign -> Customer -> What happened, as a flat record).
- Boundary: A basic record view, not the Phase 3 visual journey timeline. No funnels,
  no animated timeline, no cohort views.

8. METRIC DEFINITIONS (AGREED)
================================================================================
The following operating definitions apply to Phase 1 reporting (Section 4.9):

- Response: A customer replies to a campaign message (WhatsApp inbound detected
  by the system).
- Entered sales flow: A responding customer is routed out of the campaign sequence
  into the existing Layla sales flow.
- Conversion: The existing sales-flow definition of a completed/converted sale (as
  currently tracked in the lead system).
- Delivery rate: Delivered messages / messages sent.
- Read rate: Read receipts / delivered messages (where WhatsApp provides read data).

6. Custom AI model training or fine-tuning.

9. Nurture campaigns + context-aware nurture (Phase 2).
10. Full Customer Intelligence Profile feeding Layla context (Phase 2).
11. Visual campaign journey timeline per customer (Phase 3).
12. Pause/resume with mid-sequence state restoration; per-customer pause/restart;

13. Self-learning classification pipeline (beyond stored manual corrections).
14. Formal failed-message auto-retry rules.
15. Formal campaign-data CSV export (lead export already exists).
16. Automatic in-flight workflow resume on service restore.
17. Cleaning/mapping of a non-template external data export (the template upload
    itself is free and already live).
18. Performance optimisation / load testing beyond the current data volume.
19. Advanced backup / disaster-recovery SLA with tested drills (basic backups are
    included in 7.2).

10. MILESTONES & ACCEPTANCE
================================================================================
10.1 Milestones (indicative, for progress tracking)
- M1: Data model & campaign schema (campaign, sequence, enrolment, interaction,
      classification storage)
- M2: Campaign Creator + sequence configuration + list targeting
- M3: Follow-up / sequence progression engine (scheduled sends)
- M4: Response detection & routing into sales flow + intent classification + "not
      interested" reason capture
- M5: Manual controls (7.1) + basic customer journey view (7.3)
- M6: Campaign dashboard + performance reporting
- M7: Backups (7.2) + error/warning handling
- M8: Testing, QA, template coordination
- M9: Controlled pilot & acceptance

Milestones are for visibility and may be reported against during development.
Estimated Phase 1 duration remains 2-3 months. WhatsApp template submission to Meta
(24-48h per template) is not counted against development time.

10.2 Acceptance
- Phase 1 is considered delivered when every feature in Section 4 and Section 7
  passes its acceptance criterion.
- Each Section 4/7 feature constitutes a checklist item; the full list is the Phase 1
  acceptance checklist.
- Acceptance includes a controlled pilot against a limited number of customers/test
  numbers, to be run before final sign-off (Section 10.3).
- The final payment is due on completion of the pilot and written acceptance of
  the Phase 1 scope.

10.3 Controlled Pilot
- Before final acceptance and the final payment, the client will conduct a controlled
  pilot against a limited number of customers/test numbers.
- The pilot verifies the Phase 1 acceptance checklist in a live environment with a
  small, controlled audience.
- Pilot scope (number of test contacts, campaigns) is agreed in writing before the
  pilot begins.
- The pilot is the test-and-accept step. It does not defer the final payment:
  acceptance (including the pilot) and the final payment are the same gate.

11. CHANGE REQUESTS
================================================================================
Any feature, modification, or addition outside this frozen scope is a change
request and is handled as follows:

1. The client requests a change (verbally or in writing).
2. OWD Solutions responds in writing within 3 business days, stating whether it is
   in scope or a change request, the estimated additional cost (if any), and the
   impact on timeline (if any).
3. If accepted by the client, the change is added to the scope in writing and
   development proceeds.
4. If not accepted, the change is deferred or dropped.
5. No change request work begins until the client has approved the additional cost
   and timeline in writing.

Included without a change request:
- Bug fixes for features delivered in Phase 1 (within the 1-month warranty).
- Minor adjustments (label changes, default values, list reordering).
- Configuration changes (campaign timing, adding a template to an existing campaign type).
- Reasonable refinement of AI prompts and classification logic to improve accuracy.
- WhatsApp template submission to Meta (technical submission; content writing is
  the client's responsibility).

13.2 Warranty (1 month per phase)

  from delivery/acceptance.
- Covered: defects where delivered Phase 1 functionality does not behave as specified
  in this document - including workflow failures, incorrect campaign transitions,
  implementation-caused classification errors, implementation-caused WhatsApp
  integration failures, reporting discrepancies, and regression of previously-working
  Phase 1 functionality.
- Not covered: failures of third-party services outside OWD control (Meta/WhatsApp
  outages, OpenRouter outages, n8n platform issues), changes to data made by the
  client, and anything outside this scope.

13.3 Defect vs Change-Request
- A defect is a Phase 1 feature behaving contrary to this document's spec -> corrected
  at no additional cost within the warranty.
- A change request is new or different functionality beyond this document -> quoted
  separately.
- The test is this document: if the built system does not match Section 4/7 as written,
  it is a defect; if the client wants behaviour beyond Section 4/7, it is a change
  request.
